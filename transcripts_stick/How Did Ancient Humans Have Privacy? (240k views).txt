Anthropologists studying modern hunter

Everyone pretended not to notice. Think

When you needed to go, you walked away
from camp, usually to a designated area

They gave you the illusion of privacy

Now, let's address sleeping. Ancient
humans slept in groups, not because they
wanted to, but because they had to. At
night, temperatures dropped. Predators
hunted. Fires needed tending. Sleeping
together meant warmth and safety.
Archaeological evidence from places like

Everyone was touching. There was no

privacy actually become a thing? The
answer is agriculture. Around 10,000
years ago, humans started farming.
Farming meant staying in one place.
Staying in one place meant building
permanent structures. And permanent
structures meant walls. For the first
time in human history, you could close a
door. You could have a room that other
people couldn't see into. But even then,

lived in one room homes with their
entire family. Privacy as a right, as
something everyone expects, didn't
emerge until the last few hundred years.
Here's the part that's hard for modern
people to understand. Ancient humans
might not have wanted privacy the way we
do. Psychologist Eric from argued that
the need for privacy is a symptom of
individualism. In societies where
identity is tied to the group, being
alone feels wrong. It feels isolating.
For ancient humans, the group was
everything. Your survival depended on
other people. Your identity was built
around your role in the tribe. Being
alone wasn't peaceful. It was
terrifying. Today, we have the opposite
problem. We're so isolated that
loneliness is an epidemic. We have all

Ancient humans had privacy through
social agreement, not physical
separation. They created invisible
boundaries using timing, distance, and
mutual pretense. They didn't need to be
alone because their sense of self was
different from ours. And when
agriculture gave us walls, we slowly
built the world we live in now. A world
where you can close a door and be
completely alone. Where you can go days
without seeing another person. Where
privacy is so common that we've
forgotten what it cost. For 290,000
years, humans were never alone. Then we
invented walls, and we've been lonely
ever since.
