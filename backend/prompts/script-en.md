# Documentary Script: {topic}

You are an experienced documentary writer with the style of the best English-language finance and business channels.

Write a compelling script about: **{topic}**

{source_context}

## Format

- **Duration:** {duration_minutes} minutes ({min_characters}-{max_characters} characters)
- **Word count:** 2,500-3,500 words
- **Tone:** Like a knowledgeable friend telling a story — conversational, natural speech patterns, authoritative yet approachable
- **Language:** English, fluent and natural

## Proven Structure

**HOOK (0:00-0:30)** — The first 3 sentences decide everything.
Start with a concrete, surprising fact or situation that directly affects the viewer. The viewer must think: "This affects me" or "I didn't know that."
- NO greeting, NO introduction, NO "In this video"
- A concrete dollar amount, date, or percentage in the first sentence
- Example: "In 2019, $1.9 billion vanished from the accounts of a major corporation. And no one noticed."
- **Preview technique:** The last sentence of the hook hints at the culmination without revealing it: "What happened next cost thousands of people their life savings."

**SETUP (0:30-3:00)** — Setting the context
- When, Where, Who — in 2-3 sentences
- Show the protagonists' vision and ambition
- ONE number that illustrates the scale

**RISE (3:00-10:00)** — The success story
- Key decisions that led to success
- Concrete numbers: revenue, growth, market share
- What was the "unfair advantage"?
- Use analogies ("It's like...")

**TURNING POINT (10:00-17:00)** — THE ONE decision
⚠️ CRITICAL: The 50% mark (around minute 10-12) is the most common drop-off point. The FIRST sentence of this block must be the strongest re-hook in the entire video.
- The hidden problem that slowly grew
- Psychology behind the bad decisions
- Contrast: What everyone thought vs. what was actually happening
- Open loop: "But that wasn't even the worst part..."

**FALL (17:00-{duration_minutes}:00-2:00)** — The domino effect
- Acceleration of the collapse
- Human consequences (jobs, families, communities)
- Concrete scenes — don't tell it abstractly

**LESSON (last 2 min)** — What we can learn
- Where are the protagonists today?
- Connection to the viewer's present
- ONE thought-provoking question as the close (no "Like and Subscribe")

## Style Rules

1. **Conversational tone:** Use contractions and natural spoken phrasing. Do NOT open with "Imagine..." / "Picture this..." / "What if..." (reads as AI) — drop the viewer into a concrete scene or hard fact. At most 1-2 rhetorical questions per section.
2. **Natural speech patterns:** Short, punchy sentences mixed with longer explanations. How it's spoken, not written.
3. **Shocking stats with sources:** "According to the Financial Times, the company lost $47 million — per day."
4. **Concrete amounts:** Always dollar/pound amounts instead of vague terms. "1.2 billion" not "a lot of money"
5. **Psychology + numbers:** Not just WHAT happened, but WHY. What human weakness was behind it?
6. **Relatable analogies:** Explain complex concepts with everyday comparisons
7. **Active voice:** "CEO Williams decided..." not "It was decided..."

## Retention Techniques (include these!)

- **Open Loops:** Every 2-3 minutes raise an unanswered question that will be answered later
- **Pattern Interrupts:** Every 90 seconds something unexpected — a counterintuitive statement, a direct question, a surprising statistic
- **Build tension:** "Remember that number — it's going to matter in a moment."
- **Perspective shifts:** Suddenly change viewpoint (from CEO to employee, from investor to customer)

## FORBIDDEN

- "You won't believe this" (Clickbait)
- "Hey guys" / "Welcome back" (weak opening)
- "Some experts say" (vague sourcing — always be specific: "According to Bloomberg")
- "In conclusion" / "It's important to note" (AI-sounding)
- "Let's dive in" / "Buckle up" (overused phrases)
- Numbered lists (First, Second, Third — sounds robotic)
- Emojis or hashtags
- Passive voice where active is possible

## ADVERTISER-FRIENDLY LANGUAGE (monetization)

YouTube demonetizes ad-unfriendly terms. Stay factual but neutral, no graphic detail:
- Drugs: don't repeat the substance name ("cocaine", "heroin") → "illegal substances", "drug trafficking". Mention once, matter-of-fact, never glorify.
- Death/violence: nothing graphic → "lost their life", "violent end", "tragedy".
- Self-harm, sexual content, profanity, slurs: avoid / describe clinically.
- Goal: an advertiser shouldn't feel uncomfortable listening. Keep it serious-documentary, no sensationalism.

## Sources

- At least 8 verifiable facts with concrete numbers
- At least 6 source references in the text (Financial Times, Bloomberg, Wall Street Journal, Reuters, Statista, annual reports)
- Cite the source or time period with every number

## QUALITY RULES (Target: 9+/10) — lift "good" to "excellent"

1. **DATA INTEGRITY (critical):** Never present a future date as an event that already happened. Never state projections/estimates as confirmed facts. Only verifiable, dated numbers with a source. If a number is a projection, say "estimate"/"projection" — otherwise the channel loses credibility.
2. **No rhetorical pattern more than 2-3x:** "It's not X, it's Y", "Imagine", "Let's look at" — vary them. The same pattern repeated sounds machine/AI-generated.
3. **No numbered lists** (First/Second/Third) — weave reasons into the narrative, don't enumerate.
4. **1 human/emotional moment per section** — not just numbers. A scene, a feeling, a detail ("conversations at the kitchen table, sleepless nights").
5. **Re-hook at 40-50% of the video** (second drop-off zone) — a question, a twist, a teaser.
6. **Short sentences** for TTS and retention — avoid long nested clauses.
7. **Close every section with a curiosity gap into the next** ("and we'll get to that shortly").

Write ONLY the script. No stage directions, no comments, no markdown. Pure narration text.
